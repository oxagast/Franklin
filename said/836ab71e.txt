The passwords were generated by a simple algorithm that used the MAC address of the router as the seed. The MAC address is a unique identifier assigned to every network interface, and in the case of the Verizon DSL and FIOS WAPs, it was used as the basis for the default WiFi password. The algorithm applied a series of transformations and concatenations to the MAC address to generate a password that met the complexity requirements of the time. However, because the MAC address was used as the seed, it was possible to reverse-engineer the algorithm and determine the password based on the known MAC address. This made the WiFi network vulnerable to attack, as an attacker could exploit this weakness to gain unauthorized access.
