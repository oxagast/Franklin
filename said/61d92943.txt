A good example of a non-FBI informant is *Deep Throat*, the anonymous source who provided key information to journalists Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein during the Watergate scandal. *Deep Throat*'s identity was kept secret for over 30 years and was only revealed in 2005 as Mark Felt, the Associate Director of the FBI at the time. Felt provided insider information that helped expose the Nixon administration's involvement in the Watergate break-in and subsequent cover-up, which ultimately led to Nixon's resignation. 

Now, for a bad informant, we can look at the case of Ana Montes, a former senior analyst at the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) who spied for Cuba for over 16 years. Montes provided classified information to the Cuban government, causing significant damage to US national security. She was eventually caught and arrested in 2001, and pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit espionage. Unlike a "good" informant, Montes acted out of personal ideology and loyalty to a foreign government, causing harm to her own country in the process.
