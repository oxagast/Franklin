     What limits the number of USB devices that can be connected to a computer

The number of USB devices that can be connected to a computer is primarily limited by the number of available USB ports and the bandwidth of the USB bus. Each USB port can connect to one device, and most computers have a limited number of built-in USB ports, typically ranging from 2 to 6 ports. It is possible to expand the number of available USB ports using a USB hub, which allows multiple devices to share a single USB port on the computer.

However, even with additional USB hubs, there is still a limit to the number of devices that can be connected simultaneously. This limit is determined by the bandwidth of the USB bus, which is shared among all connected devices. USB 2.0, for example, has a maximum bandwidth of 480 Mbit/s, while USB 3.0 can provide up to 5 Gbit/s. If the combined bandwidth requirement of the connected devices exceeds the available bandwidth, performance may degrade or devices may become unusable.

Additionally, some devices, especially those that require high bandwidth or power, may not function properly when connected through a USB hub. In such cases, it may be necessary to connect them directly to a USB port on the computer to ensure proper functionality and performance.

So, to summarize, the limits on the number of USB devices that can be connected to a computer are imposed by the available USB ports, the bandwidth of the USB bus, and the specific requirements of the devices themselves.
