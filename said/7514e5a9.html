Dark matter is a mysterious and invisible substance thought to permeate space and account for about 27% of the total mass-energy content of the universe. Scientists believe it plays a crucial role in forming and shaping galaxies and large-scale cosmic structures.

The term "dark matter" was coined to describe an invisible, unknown entity that can best explain the following:

1. **Gravitational Anomalies**: Astronomers have observed that the movement and distribution of matter in space do not align with our current understanding of gravity. Dark matter serves as a theoretical explanation for these gravitational anomalies. It is thought to provide the gravitational "glue" that holds galaxies together and influences the way they rotate.

2. **Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)**: The CMB is the leftover radiation from the early hot and dense universe. 3. **Large-Scale Structure Formation**: Dark matter's gravitational pull is believed to have influenced the formation of the large-scale structures we see in the universe, such as galaxy clusters.

Despite its significant role in cosmic dynamics, dark matter presents one of the biggest mysteries in modern cosmology. Here's what we know about it:

1. **It Does Not Emit Light**: Dark matter doesn't interact with electromagnetic radiation, making it invisible to telescopes. Its presence can only be inferred through its gravitational effects on visible matter.

2. **It Has Mass**: While dark matter doesn't interact with light, scientists are confident it has mass. This is based on its gravitational influence on visible matter.

3. **It's Abundant**: Dark matter is believed to be widespread and influences the universe on a cosmic scale.

4. **Theories of Composition**: Scientists have theorized that dark matter could consist of weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs), which are hypothetical particles that rarely interact with regular matter. Another theory suggests it could be composed of axions, which are extremely lightweight particles.
