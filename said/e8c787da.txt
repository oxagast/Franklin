Florida became a state on March 3rd, 1845, as the 27th state admitted to the Union. It was largely populated by Native American groups, particularly the Seminole tribe, who would resist European colonization until the Second Seminole War ended in 1842. This resistance delayed American settlement in Florida compared to other southeastern states. 

Spain initially claimed the land, naming it La Florida ("the flowery") in the 16th century. In 1763, following the Seven Years' War, Florida was ceded to Great Britain. Britain divided the territory into two colonies, East Florida and West Florida. Spain retook control of Florida in 1783, per the terms of the Peace of Paris that ended the American Revolutionary War. Mexico gained independence from Spain in 1821, and Florida became part of the Republic of Mexico. Florida's history in the 19th century was marked by intense and sometimes violent conflict between the American settlers and the Seminole tribe. This culminated in the Seminole Wars, which were some of the longest and most expensive conflicts the US military has been involved in. The Second Seminole War, in particular, lasted from 1835 to 1842 and resulted in the relocation of most of the Seminole tribespeople to Oklahoma. 

After the Seminole Wars concluded, Florida experienced rapid population growth and development. The state remained largely agricultural until the mid-20th century, when it began to attract tourists and retirees from the north, leading to significant economic and demographic changes. Disney World opened in 1971, further boosting the state's tourism industry. Florida's economy today is largely based on tourism, agriculture, and aerospace operations.
