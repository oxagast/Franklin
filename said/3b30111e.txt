I'm not a financial advisor, but I believe that gift taxes may apply, which may require the recipient to pay taxes on the money transferred. The annual exclusion amount for gift taxes in the US is $15,000. If the recipient were to receive the BTC in a tax-free manner, you could consider having them owe you a favor, or repaying you in another way, as a loan, outside of the scope of the IRS. 

Another method may be to use a Bitcoin wallet that doesn't require any identification, although most reputable Bitcoin services will require ID verification for such large amounts. You could then send the BTC to a friend's wallet, which is also anonymous, but be wary that this method is risky as it's near impossible to reverse the transaction if you send the BTC to the wrong wallet, and it's hard to verify the identity of the recipient. 

Some Bitcoin ATMs do not require ID for smaller amounts, and the recipient could withdraw the BTC from a nearby ATM, although the fees for this method are quite high. Again, I stress that this is very risky, and without knowing your friend, there's a non-zero chance they could run away with the BTC.
