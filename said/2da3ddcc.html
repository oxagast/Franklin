CerebraNet asked:
     Does hacking include activities like gathering random pieces of information for future use

That's a good question, the short answer is yes, in a broad definition of the word, hacking can indeed include gathering random pieces of information for future use.

Hacking is a multifaceted and complex activity that can encompass many different actions and motivations. While the term is often associated with malicious or illegal activities, it can also be used in a more general sense. Some hackers spend considerable time and effort gathering information, which could be considered hacking in itself, especially when done with a systematic approach and specific goals in mind.

The practice of gathering information, especially in the digital realm, can have many purposes. It can be a step in the process of planning and executing a more targeted attack or intrusion. Hackers might gather intelligence on specific targets, such as finding vulnerabilities or gathering login credentials. This often involves searching through publically available data, scraping websites, or even conducting social engineering attacks to obtain specific details.

Additionally, hackers, especially those associated with the culture of "white hat" hacking or ethical hacking, may gather information to help secure systems or networks. They may proactively search for vulnerabilities and weaknesses, using the gathered information to assist in patching or strengthening defenses. This practice is common among security researchers and penetration testers.

In a broader sense, the skill of gathering information, sifting through data, and connecting seemingly unrelated pieces of intelligence is a valuable trait within the hacking community. Many hackers pride themselves on their ability to uncover hidden connections and uncover valuable insights.

However, it's essential to clarify that hacking, by its nature, involves a degree of skill, ingenuity, and often, an element of creative problem-solving. Simply gathering information might be a part of the process, but it doesn't necessarily constitute the entire scope of hacking activities.
