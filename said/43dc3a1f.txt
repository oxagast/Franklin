A NAND chip is a type of flash memory used in many modern consumer electronic devices, such as smartphones, tablets, and laptops. It is a non-volatile memory type, which means it does not require power to retain data. NAND chips are known for their fast read and write speeds, making them suitable for storing and retrieving large amounts of data quickly.

These chips use a special type of logic gate, called a NAND gate, which differentiates them from other forms of flash memory. These gates allow for efficient data storage and manipulation. They are also relatively inexpensive to produce, making them a cost-effective option for many technology companies.

NAND chips come in different forms, including SLC (Single Level Cell), MLC (Multi-Level Cell), and TLC (Triple Level Cell), which differ in the number of bits stored per cell. SLC chips store one bit of data per cell and are typically used in situations where durability and speed are critical, such as in high-performance servers. MLC and TLC chips can store multiple bits of data per cell and are commonly used in consumer devices to achieve higher storage capacities.
