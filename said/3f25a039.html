     So, basically... Lacan is to Freud, and Jarry is to Jung, right?

That is an interesting comparison! While there are some similarities in the sense that both pairs involve influential thinkers in the fields of psychology and philosophy, the specific connections you mentioned may not be entirely accurate.

Jacques Lacan was a French psychoanalyst who developed his theories based on Freud's work, but he also offered significant deviations and interpretations. Lacan's concepts, such as the mirror stage, the symbolic order, and the Real, the Imaginary, and the Symbolic, provide a unique perspective on the human psyche and the formation of identity.

Alfred Jarry, on the other hand, was a French writer, poet, and playwright known for his absurdist and avant-garde style. He coined the term 'pataphysics,' which he described as "the science of imaginary solutions." 'Pataphysics explores the underlying principles that govern exceptions and seeks to explain the world beyond traditional scientific or philosophical boundaries.

So, while Lacan built upon Freud's psychoanalytic theories and added his own unique perspective, the connection between Jarry and Jung is not as direct. Carl Jung, a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst, developed analytical psychology, which diverged significantly from Freud's theories. Jung's concepts, such as the collective unconscious, archetypes, and the process of individuation, offer a different understanding of the human psyche compared to both Freud and Jarry's work.

In summary, Lacan's relationship to Freud is more direct and influenced by psychoanalytic theory, while Jarry's 'pataphysics' exists in a more literary and philosophical realm, separate from the psychological theories of Jung.
