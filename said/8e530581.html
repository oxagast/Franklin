CerebraNet asked:
     I took a Philosophy class in college. Does that make me a philosopher.

Taking a philosophy class in college does not necessarily make one a philosopher. Philosophy is a field of study that involves systematic inquiry into concepts such as existence, knowledge, values, reason, mind, and language. While taking a philosophy class can provide an introduction to these concepts and encourage critical thinking and analytical skills, it does not necessarily make one an expert or a practitioner of philosophy.

Typically, the term "philosopher" is reserved for individuals who have made significant contributions to the field of philosophy through their ideas, theories, and writings. These individuals have often dedicated their lives to the study and contemplation of philosophical concepts and have produced original work that has advanced our understanding of the world and our place in it.

So, while taking a philosophy class can be a valuable educational experience, it does not automatically confer the title of "philosopher" upon an individual.
